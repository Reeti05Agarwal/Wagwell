# Abstract

Pet owners often struggle at the moments when care matters most. They can't tell whether symptoms need urgent attention. They book vets by phone without knowing fees or free slots. They lose vaccination records across clinics. And they are often shut out by English-only, small-text interfaces. This project presents Wagwell, a responsive pet-care and veterinary booking website designed around Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) principles.

The home page splits users into two paths: an urgent check that turns ticked symptoms into immediate advice and shows clinics open now, and a routine booking flow. In the booking flow, users describe their visit in a single sentence, then choose a time and a payment method. Other features include filters for the language a vet speaks, side-by-side vet comparison, a health timeline for each pet with family sharing, and passwordless OTP sign-in. A dedicated mobile layout adds a bottom tab bar within thumb reach, a raised SOS button and a settings sheet.

Nielsen's ten usability heuristics, along with Fitts's, Hick's and Miller's laws and Gestalt grouping, guided every screen. Accessibility follows WCAG 2.1 AA, with high colour contrast (up to 17:1), 44 px touch targets, a font designed for low-vision readers, three text sizes, dark mode, and English, Hindi and Marathi.

The prototype was built as an interactive design canvas and in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. A task-based usability study with five participants is proposed to measure triage time, taps needed to book, form errors and System Usability Scale scores.

# Keywords: 
Human-Computer Interaction, usability heuristics, accessibility, responsive design, veterinary booking, inclusive design
